# COG-20: Agent Admission

```
Status:      Draft
Version:     0.3
Created:     2026-08-30
Updated:     2026-10-30
Authors:     Mike Anderson
```

:::note Related specifications
This standard adds one admission class to the access rules for hosted agents in
[COG-14 §4](./COG-014#4-access-control) — a target-side policy the agent's
owner declares on the record — alongside the existing owner and capability
([COG-13](./COG-013)) classes. It introduces no new token, no new authority
primitive and no change to execution identity: it decides only whether a
principal may *ask* an agent without presenting a delegation.
:::

Agents need to converse and delegate across users — an operator's
administrative agent taking requests from a resident's assistant, two users'
assistants corresponding — and, in time, across venues. Capability delegation
([COG-13](./COG-013)) covers this in principle, but for the common standing
arrangement it is the wrong tool: the *caller* must hold and present a token
for every target, and an agent holds none. The natural authority is the target
owner's: "these principals may talk to my agent." This standard lets the owner
say so once, on the record, and has the venue honour it at the same point where
it would otherwise look for a delegation.

## Principles

- **Who may ask, nothing more.** Admission answers one question: who may ask
  this agent? Whoever may ask can ask, by any route — a native operation, MCP,
  A2A or any other. The agent then acts as itself, deciding what to do with the
  request. Its authority does not depend on who asked.
- **The target decides.** Admission is the target owner's standing policy on
  the target's record. Callers present nothing.
- **Exact, never inferred.** An allowlist names principals by DID and matches
  them exactly. No prefixes, no families: a user is not its agents, and an
  agent is not its owner.
- **The operator is a class of one.** "The venue" means the venue operator —
  the venue principal and the agents it owns — and is clearly distinguished
  from every other user the venue hosts. "Everyone on this venue" is not a
  class.
- **Anyone is a deliberate choice.** An owner MAY admit every caller, the
  venue's anonymous public principal included. Whether anonymous callers may
  ask anything at all remains the operator's decision, through the
  [public capability ceiling](./COG-010#public-capability-ceiling).
- **Talking only.** Admission covers submitting requests and messages. Reading
  an agent, changing it or controlling it still requires a delegation.
- **Provenance, not instruction.** The admitted caller is recorded as the
  initiator and shown to the agent as venue-verified fact — never as a claim
  inside the request.

## Specification

### 1. The admission policy

An agent record MAY carry an admission policy, `accepts`, in its
configuration. Its value is one of:

| Value | Admits |
|-------|--------|
| `"owner"` (default when absent) | nobody beyond the owner; other callers need a delegation |
| `"venue"` | the venue operator: principals whose user is the venue's own DID — the venue acting directly, and agents it owns |
| `"anyone"` | every caller, including the venue's public (anonymous) principal |
| an array of principal DIDs | exactly those principals: a user DID admits that user only; an agent DID (`<userDID>:g:<agentId>`, [COG-13](./COG-013)) admits that one agent only. The keyword `"venue"` MAY appear as an entry |

Any other value is malformed. A venue MUST reject a malformed policy when the
record is created or updated, and MUST treat a malformed policy it nevertheless
holds as admitting nobody.

A policy that admits callers other than the owner hands on the right to ask
the agent. When a record is created with such a policy, or updated to a different
one, the venue MUST refuse the change unless its author itself holds
`agent/request` and `agent/message` on the agent.

### 2. What admission covers

Admission applies to the abilities that mean "talk to this agent" — submitting
a request or a message (`agent/request` and `agent/message` in the ability
vocabulary of [COG-13](./COG-013)) — and to the agent record as the resource,
`<ownerDID>/g/<agentId>`. It MUST NOT extend to any other ability on that
resource (reading, writing, forking, control), nor to any path beneath it (the
agent's workspace or sessions).

### 3. Evaluation

When a principal other than the owner addresses an agent, the venue evaluates
admission at the same point at which — and before — it evaluates presented
delegations ([COG-13](./COG-013)); the two are alternatives, and either
suffices. The venue MUST evaluate admission the same way however the request
arrives: as a native operation, over MCP, over A2A
([COG-14](./COG-014)) or by any other route.

Matching is exact on the caller's acting principal DID. For `"venue"`, the test
is whether the caller's *user* — the owner for an agent principal, the principal
itself otherwise — is the venue's own DID (or its `did:web` alias). Only
`"anyone"` admits the venue's public (anonymous) principal.

Admission does not replace the caller's own authority. Asking is still the
caller's invocation of the operation that asks, so the caller must be allowed
to invoke it. An anonymous caller asks only if the venue's
[public capability ceiling](./COG-010#public-capability-ceiling) allows that
invocation.

### 4. Execution and provenance

Admission changes nothing about execution identity: the agent runs as its own
sub-principal within its owner's namespace ([COG-13](./COG-013)), with its
own authority, whoever asked. The request or message is the caller's own unit
of work. The caller follows and cancels it under its own authority, and
reaches nothing else of the owner's through it. The venue MUST record the
caller as the initiator on that unit of work.

Each request or message the agent receives carries its **provenance**, written
by the venue and never taken from the request content:

- the **caller** — the principal the request comes from;
- the **authentication record**, when the caller was identified through a
  channel binding ([COG-3 §7](./COG-003#7-channel-authentication-messaging-bindings)).

The venue MUST keep the provenance with the turn in the agent's history for as
long as it keeps the turn, and MUST present it to the agent as venue-verified
fact, distinct from the request content: the caller's user, its agent id if it
is an agent, its venue where its DID names one, and the channel through which it
was identified. The presentation MUST derive from the recorded provenance and
the agent's own identity alone, so a turn is presented the same way every time.
It MUST NOT present names or titles chosen on the platform as venue-verified
identity.

### 5. Denial

A caller that is neither the owner, admitted, nor delegated is denied exactly
as [COG-14 §4](./COG-014#4-access-control) prescribes. The denial SHOULD say
that the target's admission policy does not admit the caller.

## Security considerations

- **The agent's authority is what the owner offers everyone admitted.** The
  agent acts with the same authority whoever asked, and an agent's reading of a
  request can be talked round. An owner who trusts different callers
  differently gives them different agents.
- **Anonymous callers share one identity.** Every anonymous caller is the
  venue's one public principal, so their units of work are shared: what one
  anonymous caller may do with another's is whatever the public capability
  ceiling allows. A channel's authentication record tells them apart in
  provenance only.

## Out of scope

- Agents holding and presenting delegations of their own — the general
  mechanism that admission short-cuts.
- Cross-venue addressing of agents. When it lands, a remote caller is admitted
  by the same exact matching on the DID its venue attests for it
  ([COG-15](./COG-015)).
- Admission on other per-user resources, such as a human-in-the-loop inbox
  ([COG-16](./COG-016)) — the same shape, to be specified when needed.
